# PR #1053, round 2: the first Linux table, at f644cefae2158921cd634e8d4f06e0a19739a33e

Three unprivileged `osbench all` runs per bench under Ubuntu 24.04 (kernel 6.8.0-147-generic), run by the
orchestrator. Binaries verified against r1's linux-SHA256SUMS (outputs/*/sha.txt). Each run exits 1, because
`lan` found no peer: no wired peer existed. `nvme.*` and `energy.*` are `unmeasured` unprivileged, by design.

- table-t14.md, table-amd.md: every row's p50 per run, the median of the three, and spread = (max - min) /
  median of them. Generated by tools/table.py from outputs/.
- outputs/: the runs as the benches printed them (nothing to mask: no address, MAC, SSID or serial appears).

## crunch.all.parse

All-core parse reads 2.81 s a unit on the T14 (8 threads) and 3.09 s on the AMD bench (16), against 59 ms and
73 ms single-thread. Deflate, SHA-256 and matmul scale.

- **Not a unit definition.** A sample is one thread's `syn::parse_file` of the whole generated program
  (crunch.rs `slice`). The corpus is built once and read shared. **Not a measurement bug either:** units_per_s
  falls in proportion, from 16.9 to 2.8 on the T14 and from 13.7 to 5.1 on the AMD bench.
- **Measured: a parse unit is 588,862 allocation and free pairs.** parse-allocs/ counts them with a
  counting global allocator around the same syn 2.0.117 features and the same corpus generator, on the
  development Mac (output.txt).
- **Measured in the same runs: the Linux build's allocator collapses under threads.**
  `mem.malloc_churn.16`:
  - T14: 33.6 M pairs/s on 1 thread, 3.2 M/s total on 8.
  - AMD: 28.5 M/s on 1 thread, 1.76 M/s total on 16.
  - The 4096 B rows behave the same way.
  The binary is static musl, whose malloc serialises on one lock.
- **Inferred, not measured:** at the churn rows' contended cost of about 2.5 to 9 us a pair per thread, a
  parse's allocations alone take 1.5 to 5 s. That brackets the T14's 2.8 s. The AMD bench's 3.1 s sits at the
  bracket's low end, which fits parse threads computing between allocations where churn threads only allocate.
  The figure is the allocator's under contention as much as the CPU's. ToyOS std's dlmalloc also takes one
  lock, so the ToyOS figure will measure the same thing. The row stays and the crunch module header says so
  (75878e957).

## Rows to read with care

Spread at or over 10% across the three runs (from the tables):
- **T14:** the futex storm drains, `pingpong.pipe.same_cpu` (70%), `fair.flat.total` (42%),
  `mem.memcpy.4194304` (21%), and `tail.*`.
- **AMD:** far noisier, under schedutil with acpi-cpufreq and no EPP. Examples: `cpu.chunk` (67%),
  `process.spawn_wait` (171%), `pipe.throughput.65536` (73%; run 3 read 934 against about 2,400),
  `time.timer_1ms.late` (57%), the futex storms, and `proc.*`.
