## 1. CBS, GRUB, H-CBS, Linux SCHED_DEADLINE and the dl_server

### 1.1 Constant Bandwidth Server (Abeni & Buttazzo, RTSS 1998) and its hard variant
- The CBS serves soft and multimedia tasks "without jeopardizing the a priori guarantee of hard real-time activities": hard tasks are admitted on WCET and minimum interarrival, soft tasks on mean parameters, and "each task will never exceed a predefined bandwidth, independently of its actual requests." The paper won the RTSS 2021 Influential Paper award. Abstract text via the search index of https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/7299 and https://retis.santannapisa.it/~giorgio/paps/awards/ToT2021-98.pdf (award certificate only; the 1998 PDF itself was not retrievable: the Berkeley copy returns 404). [CLAIMED by source]
- A reservation (Q, D, P) gives its tasks a virtual processor of speed α = Q/P; "each task running in a reservation with bandwidth α ≤ 1 behaves ... as if it were executing alone on a virtual processor with speed α times the one of the physical processor". https://retis.santannapisa.it/~tommaso/publications/RTNS-2017.pdf (Casini, Abeni, Biondi, Cucinotta, Buttazzo, RTNS 2017) [CLAIMED by source; follows from EDF theory under the stated model]
- A server is a *hard* reservation when it guarantees both bandwidth α and a bounded worst-case service delay Δ. For the H-CBS with D = P, Biondi et al. 2014 fixed the wakeup rule (`wakeup(q,d,t) = min(t, d − q/α)`, budget always recharged to Q) "to guarantee a bounded service delay of 2(P − Q)". Same URL. [PROVED by source (reports Biondi, Melani, Bertogna SIES 2014)]
- The classic H-CBS formulation "is affected by a schedulability issue whenever the H-CBS is adopted in conjunction to other scheduling mechanisms that can introduce blocking times (e.g., non-preemptive sections, locking protocols, etc.)" (Biondi et al. 2014), and is "not compatible" with suspension-oblivious analysis of self-suspending tasks (Biondi et al. 2015, fixed by H-CBS-SO). Same URL. [PROVED by source (reported)]
- All H-CBS variants before 2017 assumed implicit deadlines (D = P). Constrained deadlines (D < P) break the utilisation-based meta-rules; the density test Σ Q/D ≤ 1 is "only sufficient ... and highly pessimistic". The paper gives three H-CBS-D algorithms with proved worst-case properties, motivated partly by "on-going developments of the SCHED_DEADLINE scheduling class of the Linux kernel". Same URL. [PROVED by source for the algorithms; simulations: MEASURED by source in simulation only]
- Exact EDF test for constrained-deadline reservations is the Processor Demand Criterion: Σ dbf_i(t) ≤ t at every absolute deadline up to L*; pseudo-polynomial when U < 1. A linear-time sufficient test (Theorem 2.2) exists. Same URL. [PROVED by source (PDC from Baruah et al.)]

### 1.2 GRUB (Lipari & Baruah, ECRTS 2000) and its Linux form
- The ECRTS 2000 paper itself was not retrieved. The multicore successors (parallel and sequential reclaiming under global EDF) are proved correct in Abeni, Lipari, Parri, Sun 2015/2016: https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01984 [PROVED by source (claimed in abstract; proofs not read by me)]
- Linux's GRUB (`SCHED_FLAG_RECLAIM`): three states ActiveContending / ActiveNonContending / Inactive; an "inactive timer" fires at the 0-lag time after a task blocks; the running task's runtime is depleted at a rate scaled by the active (non-reclaimable) bandwidth over Umax, so a task can run into bandwidth its blocked peers do not use. Umax is the RT capacity (default 95%). https://docs.kernel.org/scheduler/sched-deadline.html [CLAIMED by source]
- GRUB-PA: with schedutil, Linux lowers the CPU frequency to the minimum that still meets admitted deadlines; ARM only. Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]

### 1.3 Linux SCHED_DEADLINE
- Per-task (runtime, deadline, period); CBS wakeup rule: if the current deadline has passed or `remaining_runtime / (deadline − now) > runtime / period`, reset deadline to now + D and refill runtime; otherwise keep both. Depleted task is throttled until its deadline, then replenished (deadline += period, runtime += runtime). `SCHED_FLAG_DL_OVERRUN` sends SIGXCPU on overrun. https://docs.kernel.org/scheduler/sched-deadline.html [CLAIMED by source]
- Scheduling is global EDF per root domain. With total utilisation < M, global EDF gives a *bounded tardiness* (formula in the doc) but **does not** guarantee all deadlines; Dhall's effect lets M+1 tasks with total utilisation near 1 miss deadlines. Sufficient global test for D = P: Σ C_i/P_i ≤ M − (M − 1)·U_max. Partitioned/uniprocessor EDF with D = P is exact at U ≤ 1. Same URL. [PROVED by source (cites the literature)]
- Admission control is a utilisation test only, at `sched_setattr()`: Σ runtime/period ≤ M × (sched_rt_runtime_us / sched_rt_period_us), default 95% per CPU in the root domain. Deadline tasks cannot fork; their affinity mask cannot be narrower than the root domain (partitioning needs cpusets/root domains). Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]
- The doc publishes no measurements. Inheritance is listed only as future work ("refinements to deadline inheritance"). Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]

### 1.4 The dl_server (fair server) replacing RT throttling
- RT throttling capped RT at 95% even when nothing lower was waiting, so it could "force the system idle", which LWN calls "an unwanted artifact". The deadline server is a SCHED_DEADLINE entity that "declares it needs 5% of the CPU" and runs lower-priority (fair) tasks with it; "if the deadline server doesn't need it, it will simply not be runnable". A deferred start (run only once fair tasks starve, ultimately at zero-laxity) keeps it from preempting well-behaved RT work needlessly. Patches by Daniel Bristot de Oliveira, Peter Zijlstra, Juri Lelli. https://lwn.net/Articles/934415/ (Corbet, 2023-06-12) [CLAIMED by source]
- Peter Zijlstra argued for boosting CFS instead of throttling RT, and noted IRQ time is not accounted against RT throttling. https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2306.1/06441.html (thread on "sched/fair: Implement starvation monitor") [CLAIMED by source]
- Merged (tip sched/core, 2024-07-29, in Linux 6.12): "Now that fair_server exists, we no longer need RT bandwidth control unless RT_GROUP_SCHED. Enable fair_server with parameters equivalent to RT throttling." The diff sets 50 ms runtime per 1000 ms period, deferred. https://patchew.org/linux/172224924216.2215.1362872184696992440.tip-bot2@tip-bot2/ [CLAIMED by source]
- A follow-up ("Less agressive dl_server handling") fixed a benchmark regression caused by stopping/starting the server too eagerly; now disabled only after a full period without fair tasks. Reported in the review thread around https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2411.3/04310.html [CLAIMED by source]
- **Measured regression:** with a 6.12-based kernel on a big.LITTLE Android device, the fair server's runtime was charged in *capacity/frequency-scaled* time, so "after 50ms of real time, we've only accounted ~238us against the fair servers runtime", and RT tasks on the LITTLE CPUs were blocked for seconds. Fix: charge reservations in wall time. https://patchew.org/linux/175162039765.406.4702062609825995302.tip-bot2@tip-bot2/ [CLAIMED by source]. John Stultz: cyclictest (`-t -a -p99 -m`) showed "100ms to *multi-second* delays of the RT prio 99 threads" before, "single digit ms range" max after. https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/2506.1/09834.html [MEASURED by source]
- **Accounting bug in the design:** after RT throttling was removed, the fair servers' 5% was still charged against the 95% RT pool, leaving SCHED_DEADLINE tasks and other dl-servers 90%. The 2025 fix stops counting fair servers in RT admission and caps each at BW_UNIT − max_rt_bw. Yuri Andriaccio, v4 2025-09-03. https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2509.0/04626.html [CLAIMED by source]
- Note: the fair server bounds *only* SCHED_FIFO/RR against fair; SCHED_DEADLINE is bounded by its own admission. Two mechanisms (fixed-priority RT + EDF reservations) still coexist in Linux; the dl_server is a reservation that sits above FIFO in the class order and is deferred to zero laxity. [INFERENCE - mine, from the above]


## 2. seL4 MCS: scheduling contexts as capabilities

Primary: Lyons, McLeod, Almatary, Heiser, "Scheduling-Context Capabilities: A Principled, Light-Weight Operating-System Mechanism for Managing Time", EuroSys 2018. https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Lyons_MAH_18.pdf

### 2.1 Model
- Criticality, time-sensitivity and trust are distinct; "most OSes only have a single parameter for controlling both attributes, priority". Network interrupts are time-sensitive but low-criticality; flight control the opposite. [CLAIMED by source]
- A scheduling context (SC) is a first-class kernel object reached by a capability (scCap): "a capability to time". It holds budget C and period T (C ≤ T, U = C/T), the core it grants access to, and a bounded refill list. A thread runs only while bound to an SC with budget. [CLAIMED by source]
- "Setting budgets is admission control and requires appropriate privilege": anyone holding Untyped memory can create SCs, but only a holder of the per-core `sched_control` capability can give one a non-zero budget. Admission policy (how much overcommit, which test) is user-level, not the kernel's. [CLAIMED by source]
- An SC is tied to one core; migration is user-level policy (rebind to an SC of another core). Donation is intra-core only; a passive server called from another core has its execution context migrated to the caller's core, an overhead "which must be included in any schedulability analysis (or avoided by design)". [CLAIMED by source]
- Priority stays a *thread* attribute, orthogonal to the SC (unlike Fiasco.OC). The kernel keeps fixed-priority scheduling; within a priority, round-robin. Rationale: overcommitted fixed-priority systems degrade predictably ("If the sum of utilisations of threads at priority ≥ P is below the utilisation bound, then all those threads will meet their deadlines, while any thread with priority < P may miss"), while EDF under overload "victimises whatever thread happens to be furthest from its deadline". EDF is implemented at user level on one priority. [CLAIMED by source]
- Time is "divisible but not fungible" (real-estate analogy): time capabilities "cannot support hierarchical delegation without loss, and cannot be recursively virtualised". [CLAIMED by source]
- Budget enforcement uses sporadic servers (Sprunt 1989, implementation after Stanovic et al. 2010): "during any time interval not exceeding the period, no more than C can be consumed". The refill queue is bounded per SC (`extra_refills`); excess is merged/discarded; with one refill it degrades to a polling server. A full SC (C = T) is just a round-robin time slice. [CLAIMED by source]
- Timeout exceptions: a thread may name a timeout-fault endpoint; on budget expiry the kernel sends it an IPC. Handler policies: one-off emergency budget, raise budget, criticality switch, kill/suspend, or roll the server back and reply to the client. [CLAIMED by source]
- Kernel invariant: every thread in a scheduling or endpoint queue has enough budget to exit the kernel; threads are charged only on SC change; scheduler precision is "twice the kernel's WCET". The kernel became tickless. [CLAIMED by source]

### 2.2 Passive servers and SC donation over IPC
- "A client invoking a server can pass its SC along, so the server executes on the client's SC, until it replies ... This ensures that time consumed by the server is billed to the client requesting the work." A passive server has no SC of its own. Active servers (own SC) execute "for free" from the client's view; both are allowed. [CLAIMED by source]
- Old L4 time-slice donation is called "unprincipled and hard, if not impossible, to analyse": the slice could expire in the server, which then ran on its own slice; no consistent accounting, no temporal isolation. [CLAIMED by source]
- Passive servers give IPCP (immediate priority ceiling) for free: the server thread is set to the ceiling priority of its clients; the capability to call it is what bounds the set of callers, so "the requirement that all lockers' priorities are known a priori, is easy to enforce in a capability-based system". [CLAIMED by source]
- Explicit Reply ("Resume") objects record the donation chain so a donated SC returns to its owner across nested passive servers and concurrent requests; "There are no limits on SC donation; this process can be nested indefinitely." [CLAIMED by source]
- Against helping/inheritance: "helping schemes, such as PIP or bandwidth inheritance, as implemented in Fiasco.OC, make the waiting client pay for another client's contract violation", and force every client to budget for the server's full WCET. With timeout exceptions a server can abort a request whose client's budget ran out, so "clients can only be blocked for the largest budget of the other clients, plus the short cleanup time". Helping can be layered on top at user level. [CLAIMED by source]
- A Notification can carry an SC and donate it to a passive server waiting on it (for IRQ completion and other signals). [CLAIMED by source]

### 2.3 Measurements (EuroSys 2018)
- IPC fastpath overhead vs baseline seL4, cycles: x64 Haswell `call` 448→455 (+1%), `replyRecv` 431→450 (+4%); ARM Cortex-A9 `call` 288→350 (+21%), `replyRecv` 313→339 (+8%). IRQ latency +8% (x64) / +13% (ARM). A reschedule to a higher-priority thread ("schedule") +38% (x64, 1512→2100) / +45% (ARM), attributed to reading and reprogramming the timer and the sporadic refill logic. [MEASURED by source]
- Temporal isolation: a Linux VM running `yes` at high priority with a 10 ms period and budget 1..10 ms; a low-priority UDP echo server's maximum latency tracks the VM's budget exactly up to 9 ms and blows out only at a full 10 ms budget. [MEASURED by source]
- Redis/YCSB-A on x86: seL4-base 138.7 k ops/s vs seL4-MCS 138.5 k ops/s (APIC); throughput scales linearly with the bandwidth left by a CPU hog. [MEASURED by source]
- Timeout-handler costs on x86 (cold cache, max): rollback 4.96 µs, emergency budget 3.04 µs, extend 1.46 µs, kill 4.75 µs. [MEASURED by source]
- A user-level EDF scheduler on seL4 MCS was faster than LITMUS^RT's in-kernel C-EDF (Feather-Trace) on the same thread sets. [MEASURED by source]
- Criticality switch of 8 threads: in-kernel < 2 µs x64, ~12 µs ARM (cold); user level is linear in threads and slower but adequate. [MEASURED by source]
- The MCS changes add ~2,000 SLOC (14%) to the kernel. [MEASURED by source]

### 2.4 What is verified
- 2018: "Its functionality has been formally specified ... verification of the code base is in progress." Lyons et al. above. [CLAIMED by source]
- 2026 (page fetched 2026-10-12): "The proofs for AARCH64_MCS/ARM_MCS (mixed-criticality extensions to real-time seL4 features) are in progress"; RISCV64_MCS is listed as an ongoing configuration. No MCS configuration is listed as having a completed functional-correctness proof. https://docs.sel4.systems/projects/sel4/verified-configurations.html [CLAIMED by source]
- The seL4 Summit 2024 verification talk calls MCS a large invasive change (~15% code increase, new SC and Reply objects). https://sel4.systems/Summit/2024/slides/sel4-verification.pdf (seen via search index only) [CLAIMED by source]
- So: the *mechanism* is specified and partly verified; schedulability is not proved by the kernel at all (admission is user-level policy). [INFERENCE - mine]

## 3. Fuchsia / Zircon

- Hybrid per-CPU scheduler: each CPU runs its own instance with three run queues (critical deadline, deadline, fair), augmented WAVL trees, O(log n). "Deadline tasks always take precedence over eligible fair tasks." Deadline selection picks "the eligible deadline task with the earliest finish time" (EDF per CPU). https://fuchsia.dev/docs/concepts/kernel/kernel_scheduling [CLAIMED by source]
- **No admission control in the kernel**: "If the total deadline demand of a processor exceeds 100%, stochastic deadline misses are expected" and the system behaves as if periods were stretched by the overload factor. *Critical* deadline tasks get absolute priority under oversubscription, and "total critical utilization should never exceed the limits of the processor" or the kernel emits an oops. Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]
- Fair band: weights, virtual finish time order (WFQ-like), period stretched to give every thread a minimum granularity; lower-weight threads "guaranteed timely execution to prevent starvation and unbounded priority inversion" — but nothing reserves fair time against deadline threads beyond the 100% feasibility expectation. Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]
- Placement: wakeup CPU chosen by affinity, then last CPU, then idle state; idle CPUs steal work, preferring the local cluster. Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]
- Deadline parameters: `0 < capacity <= relative_deadline <= period`. https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/fuchsia/+/HEAD/zircon/system/public/zircon/syscalls/scheduler.h (via search index) [CLAIMED by source]
- Profiles are kernel objects; "creating a profile requires the root resource" (now a system resource `ZX_RSRC_SYSTEM_PROFILE_BASE` plus job policy `ZX_POL_NEW_PROFILE`); "profiles may be delegated freely" and applied with `zx_object_set_profile()`. https://fuchsia.dev/docs/reference/kernel_objects/profile , https://fuchsia.dev/reference/syscalls/profile_create [CLAIMED by source]
- Profile inheritance through owned futexes: "Deadline profiles must always be inheritable"; `ZX_PROFILE_INFO_FLAG_CRITICAL` propagates to the futex owner. https://fuchsia.dev/reference/syscalls/profile_create [CLAIMED by source]. OwnedWaitQueue composes the effective profiles of all waiters, propagates transitively and refuses ownership changes that would create a cycle (`ZX_ERR_BAD_STATE`). https://docs.fuchsia.dev/cpp/GlobalNamespace/_ZTV14OwnedWaitQueue.html [CLAIMED by source]
- The inheritance arithmetic (`RecomputeEffectiveProfile`): a deadline owner gets `min(own_util + Σ inherited_util, kThreadUtilizationMax)` and `min(own_deadline, inherited_min_deadline)`; a fair owner with deadline waiters *becomes* a deadline thread with the inherited utilisation and minimum deadline; a fair owner with fair waiters gets `own_weight + Σ waiter weights`. This is bandwidth inheritance (BWI) in the Lamastra/Lipari/Abeni sense, not plain priority inheritance. https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/fuchsia/+/refs/heads/main/zircon/kernel/kernel/scheduler_state.cc [CLAIMED by source (code); BWI label is INFERENCE - mine]
- **Channel calls**: in January 2026 Fuchsia began making `zx_channel_call` propagate bandwidth to the server thread: "This chain of CLs modifies channel calls to propagate bandwidth, so that client threads that are blocked by server threads propagate their priority" (plumbing an OwnedWaitQueue through ChannelDispatcher), followed by "This change forces bandwidth inheritance during channel calls, so that we propagate bandwidth from threads that have inheritance disabled (which by default includes all user space fair threads)", plus an `UnblockSynchronous` to cut reschedules on the call path. Bug 42182908. https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/integration/+/c4c3168d90144c442930097d6f6826770387c481 , https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/integration/+/ba88d6219a28ce5d9ed1b9e8dcab7b5d29507a86 [CLAIMED by source]
- Media threads request elevated profiles by *role name* through `fuchsia.media.ProfileProvider.RegisterHandlerWithCapacity(name, period, capacity)`: "capacity should be a good faith estimate based on the worst case runtime the thread requires each period"; it is "planned / measured against the highest performance processor(s)" and "The system will automatically adjust the effective capacity to account for slower processors and operating points"; it returns the period and capacity actually applied. Roles are mapped to parameters per product. https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/fuchsia/+/HEAD/sdk/fidl/fuchsia.media/profile_provider.fidl [CLAIMED by source]. `fuchsia.scheduler.RoleManager.SetRole` is the newer general form: "The exact parameters of the role are system dependent and may vary based on device-specific tuning". https://docs.fuchsia.dev/cpp/fuchsia/scheduler/_ZTVN7fuchsia9scheduler11RoleManagerE.html (via search index) [CLAIMED by source]
- No published Fuchsia scheduler measurements were found. [INFERENCE - mine, absence of evidence after searching]


## 4. Apple XNU: realtime threads, Clutch/Edge, audio workgroups

### 4.1 Realtime (time-constraint) threads
- `THREAD_TIME_CONSTRAINT_POLICY {period, computation, constraint, preemptible}`: "A real-time thread tells the scheduler that it needs to run for A cycles out of the next B cycles"; `constraint` is the maximum span from start to end of the computation and "must be larger than the value for computation"; `preemptible` says whether the computation may be split. Units are Mach absolute time. "The kernel will make every effort to honor the request, but since this is soft real-time, it cannot be guaranteed"; a thread that "lies blatantly about its requirements and behaves in a compute-bound fashion ... may be demoted to the priority of a normal thread". "This is commonly used when interacting with CoreAudio." POSIX pthreads cannot reach the RT band. https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Darwin/Conceptual/KernelProgramming/scheduler/scheduler.html (archived Kernel Programming Guide) [CLAIMED by source]
- Fail-safe demotion in source: `TH_SFLAG_FAILSAFE` is part of `TH_SFLAG_DEMOTED_MASK`; in xnu-4570 `max_unsafe_quanta` defaults to 800 and `max_unsafe_computation = max_unsafe_quanta × std_quantum`, with the demotion lasting `sched_safe_duration = 2 × max_unsafe_quanta × std_quantum`. https://opensource.apple.com/source/xnu/xnu-4570.1.46/osfmk/kern/sched_prim.c.auto.html , https://opensource.apple.com/source/xnu/xnu-6153.41.3/osfmk/kern/thread.h.auto.html (via search index; values may have changed since) [CLAIMED by source]
- Current XNU (xnu-12377, macOS 26.5) `sched_rt.c`: per-pset realtime run queues, one queue per RT priority, each kept "in thread deadline order"; `rt_runq_dequeue` may pick an earlier-deadline thread from a lower RT priority over the highest-priority one when `ed.computation + hi.computation + rt_deadline_epsilon < hi.constraint` (a laxity check), unless `sched_rt_runq_strict_priority`; `rt_deadline_epsilon` = 100 µs; `rt_constraint_threshold` = 4 ms marks a queue "low latency" and triggers backup IPIs to extra CPUs; RT computation clamped to [50 µs, 50 ms]; preemption targets the pset CPU running the furthest deadline; on Edge platforms RT threads "spill" to other psets along an edge-weighted order. **No admission control or utilisation cap is visible.** https://cs.iossec.tech/xnu/latest/source/osfmk/kern/sched_rt.c , https://cs.iossec.tech/xnu/latest/source/osfmk/kern/sched_rt.h [CLAIMED by source (code)]
- An older (2010) reading of XNU 1504 describes RT as global EDF: deadline = now + constraint, `choose_processor()` comparing priority then deadline. https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/1004.2/02100.html [CLAIMED by source]

### 4.2 Audio workgroups (macOS 11+)
- "An audio workgroup is a collection of real-time threads that work together to produce audio by a common deadline while spanning multiple processes, including the audio server and client apps and plug-ins." The audio server's I/O thread "informs the kernel of the beginning and end of each work cycle"; "The system automatically joins the client app's real-time thread to the audio device's workgroup ... tells the kernel that both threads are working together with a common deadline"; "Only real-time threads can join"; the system uses workgroups "to observe CPU usage of real-time threads so that it can better balance the competing interests of power consumption and performance". https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/audiotoolbox/understanding-audio-workgroups.json (JSON of https://developer.apple.com/documentation/audiotoolbox/understanding-audio-workgroups) [CLAIMED by source]
- So Apple's answer to "the audio path crosses several processes" is a *named, cross-process deadline group* with explicit interval start/finish, which feeds the performance controller (frequency/cluster), not budget donation. [INFERENCE - mine]

### 4.3 Clutch / Edge (timeshare)
- Three-level hierarchy: root buckets (≈ QoS classes) → clutch buckets (thread groups) → threads. Root buckets are chosen by **EDF** with deadline = now + WCEL[bucket], advanced by WCEL on each selection: WCEL FG 0, IN 37.5, DF 75, UT 150, BG 250 ms; FIXPRI/AboveUI special-cased against FG by comparing highest clutch buckets. Warp gives a bounded burst advantage to higher buckets (FG 8, IN 4, DF 2, UT 1, BG 0 ms). Starvation avoidance: a lower bucket picked by EDF while a higher one is runnable gets a window of one quantum. Quanta FIXPRI/FG 10, IN 8, DF 6, UT 4, BG 2 ms. Rationale: thread-level accounting "incentivizes creating more threads"; Mach timesharing has "poor isolation"; priority scheduling leads to "priority inflation across the platform". https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/doc/scheduler/sched_clutch_edge.md [CLAIMED by source]
- Edge (AMP): CLPC gives each thread group a preferred cluster; per-QoS migration graph with edge weights = scheduling-latency delta; "Scheduling-Latency(QoS) = Cumulative Higher QoS Load(QoS) * Avg. Execution Latency(QoS)" (EWMAs); "Migrate 'down' only"; idle CPUs steal (foreign runnable, native, then rebalance running). Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]
- The doc publishes no measurements. Same URL. [CLAIMED by source]

## 5. Windows MMCSS; Linux PipeWire/JACK with rtkit; audio periods

### 5.1 Windows MMCSS
- MMCSS lets multimedia threads get "prioritized access to CPU resources ... without denying CPU resources to lower-priority applications". Threads opt in with `AvSetMmThreadCharacteristics("Pro Audio" | "Audio" | ...)`. `SystemResponsiveness` "determines the percentage of CPU resources that should be guaranteed to low-priority tasks" (default 20; rounded to multiples of 10; values <10 or >100 clamp to 20; 100 disables MMCSS). Categories: High 23-26 ("lower than only certain system-level tasks ... designed for Pro Audio"), Medium 16-22 (foreground app), Low 8-15; "1-7: These threads have used their quota of CPU resource. They can continue to run if no low-priority threads are ready to run." `Clock Rate` was a guaranteed timer rate in Vista; "Starting with Windows 7 ... this guarantee was removed to reduce system power consumption." https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/multimedia-class-scheduler-service (doc dated 2025-07-14) [CLAIMED by source]
- MMCSS is fixed-priority boosting policed by a user-mode service: a quota per category, demotion to 1-7 when exhausted, and a reserved fraction for the rest. It is a throttle, not a reservation with a deadline. [INFERENCE - mine]

### 5.2 Linux: PipeWire, rtkit
- PipeWire's module-rt gives "real-time priorities to processing threads"; it needs `RLIMIT_RTPRIO ≥ rt.prio`, else falls back to the realtime portal or RTKit over D-Bus; `rt.time.soft/hard` are "The amount of CPU time an RT thread can consume without doing any blocking calls before the kernel kills the thread" (RLIMIT_RTTIME). https://docs.pipewire.org/page_module_rt.html [CLAIMED by source]
- PipeWire graph defaults: `default.clock.rate = 48000`, `default.clock.quantum = 1024`, `min-quantum = 32`, `max-quantum = 8192`; quantum requests "are rounded down to a power of two"; one RT data loop by default runs all nodes. https://docs.pipewire.org/page_man_pipewire_conf_5.html [CLAIMED by source]
- RealtimeKit: "a D-Bus system service that changes the scheduling policy of user processes/threads to SCHED_RR"; "Only clients with RLIMIT_RTTIME set will get RT scheduling"; SCHED_RESET_ON_FORK required; PolicyKit authorisation; rate-limited; a priority ceiling; a **canary watchdog** that "automatically demotes all real-time threads to SCHED_OTHER" when it detects starvation. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heftig/rtkit/master/README [CLAIMED by source]
- So the deployed Linux desktop audio stack uses SCHED_FIFO/RR (fixed priority, no budget, no deadline) guarded by an RLIMIT_RTTIME kill, a userland watchdog and the kernel's 5% fair server — three policing layers around a mechanism with no notion of time. [INFERENCE - mine]

### 5.3 Audio periods as deadlines (arithmetic)
- At 48 kHz a period of N frames is N/48 ms: 32 → 0.667 ms, 64 → 1.333 ms, 128 → 2.667 ms, 256 → 5.333 ms, 1024 → 21.33 ms. With double buffering the deadline for producing period k+1 is roughly one period after its start, so a 128-frame stream asks for a few hundred µs of CPU with a ~2.67 ms relative deadline and period. [INFERENCE - mine, arithmetic]
- Fuchsia frames the same request as (role, period, capacity) measured on the fastest core and scaled by the system (section 3); Apple as (period, computation, constraint) plus a workgroup interval. [INFERENCE - mine, summarising sections 3-4]

## 6. Priority inversion over IPC: inheritance, BWI, proxy execution, donation

### 6.1 Bandwidth inheritance (BWI, M-BWI)
- BWI (Lamastra, Lipari, Abeni RTSS 2001; Lipari, Lamastra, Abeni TSE 2004) "combines the Constant Bandwidth Server with Priority Inheritance to achieve bandwidth isolation in open systems": "The server of a lock-owner task can leverage not only its own budget to complete the critical section, but also the inherited budgets of servers possibly blocked on the lock it is owning"; "tasks that are not involved in the resource contention are not influenced". The problem it solves: a server exhausting its budget inside a critical section makes all waiters wait for its recharge; letting it run negative breaks analysis. Faggioli, Lipari, Cucinotta, "Analysis and implementation of the multiprocessor bandwidth inheritance protocol", Real-Time Systems 48(6), 2012. https://retis.santannapisa.it/~tommaso/publications/RTSJ-2012.pdf [CLAIMED by source (summarising Lamastra 2001)]
- M-BWI Theorem 1: a reservation set schedulable when resource access is ignored stays schedulable under M-BWI ("every server always respects its scheduling deadline"). Theorem 2: a hard task with server (Q_i ≥ C_i + I_i, P_i ≤ T_i) never misses, where I_i is the interference from *interacting* tasks only (the set Ψ_i); non-interacting tasks cannot inherit S_i. When the owner runs on another CPU the waiter's server busy-waits (spins) — on multiprocessors M-BWI combines blocking, migration and spinning. Interference bound algorithm is super-exponential (off-line, small n). Same URL. [PROVED by source]
- Overhead on LITMUS^RT "almost negligible for many practical applications"; no parameters needed from the user ("Like the Priority Inheritance Protocol, M-BWI does not require the user to specify any additional parameter"). Same URL. [MEASURED by source (overhead); CLAIMED (characterisation)]

### 6.2 seL4: donation + timeout exceptions, not inheritance
- See 2.2: passive servers run on the caller's SC (billing the caller), IPCP via server priority = client ceiling, and timeout exceptions to abort a request when the donated budget runs out; Lyons et al. reject BWI/PIP in the kernel because they "make the waiting client pay for another client's contract violation". https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Lyons_MAH_18.pdf [CLAIMED by source]

### 6.3 Fuchsia: bandwidth inheritance through futexes, and (2026) through channel calls
- See section 3: effective profile = base + Σ inherited utilisation, min deadline, Σ fair weights; channel calls now propagate bandwidth to the server thread, forced even for fair threads with inheritance off. [CLAIMED by sources in section 3]

### 6.4 Linux proxy execution
- "With proxy execution, the waiting process's entire scheduling context is loaned to the resource-holding process": a task blocked on a mutex stays on the run queue; when picked, "the link to the process holding the mutex will be followed" and the owner runs in its place, charged to the waiter. Motivation: "Deadline tasks do not use priorities, but they can still be blocked on resources held by other processes", so PI has nothing to inherit. LWN, Corbet, 2026-07-16. https://lwn.net/Articles/1082717/ [CLAIMED by source]
- Stultz's 2022 RFC splits task state into a *scheduling context* (class, priority, deadline params) and an *execution context* (affinity etc.); only the scheduling context transfers. https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/2210.0/01894.html (via search index) [CLAIMED by source]
- Mainline status: initial support merged in 6.17 (same-runqueue only, behind CONFIG_EXPERT / `sched_proxy_exec=`). https://lwn.net/Articles/1031713 (6.17 merge window part 1, via search index), https://patchew.org/linux/175266115435.406.15916021190678492115.tip-bot2@tip-bot2/ [CLAIMED by source]. Cross-CPU "donor migration" still under review in March 2026 (v26), with testing limited by an unrelated `__pick_eevdf()` NULL issue to ~4 h runs. https://lkml.iu.edu/2603.3/10127.html [CLAIMED by source]. An earlier version showed "~3-5% regressions in some cases" (K Prateek Nayak). https://lwn.net/Articles/1013969/ [MEASURED by source (reported)]. Proxy execution covers kernel mutexes only — not futexes or IPC. [CLAIMED by sources above; "not futexes" is INFERENCE - mine from their scope statements]

### 6.5 Which solves the microkernel server problem [INFERENCE - mine]
- The audio path crosses several userland servers (client → mixer → driver), so the inversion is over *IPC*, not mutexes. Three shapes exist:
  1. **Donation (seL4 passive servers)**: server has no time of its own; the caller's reservation is the only budget it can run on; billing is exact; nested calls chain through reply objects; no analysis of other clients' critical sections is needed for *isolation*, but a shared single-threaded server still serialises clients, so a client's blocking is bounded by the *other* clients' budgets (or the timeout-abort path). Requires the server to be passive and core-local (cross-core calls migrate the server).
  2. **Bandwidth inheritance (BWI/M-BWI, Fuchsia channels)**: server keeps its own reservation and additionally runs on the waiters'; isolation proven for non-interacting tasks; interacting tasks must budget I_i for others' critical sections; works for active, multi-threaded servers and for the case where the server is already busy when the call arrives.
  3. **Proxy execution (Linux)**: the mechanism that implements (2) generically inside one scheduler — the blocked task stays queued and lends its whole scheduling context. It is BWI's implementation strategy, applied to locks.
- Donation is the cleanest fit when the server is per-client-request and passive (a codec, a mixer stage invoked synchronously). Inheritance/proxy is required when the server is already running another request or is active (has its own event loop, timers, IRQs). A microkernel needs both: donate when the server is idle and waiting, inherit (proxy) when the server is busy, and bill the caller for the time either way.


### 6.6 Measured: reservations vs SCHED_FIFO for audio (Android)
- Balsini, Cucinotta, Abeni, Fernandes, Burk, Bellasi, Rasmussen, "Energy-Efficient Low-latency Audio on Android", JSS 152, 2019. https://retis.santannapisa.it/~tommaso/publications/Elsevier-JSS-2019.pdf
  - Android low-latency audio ran its callback under SCHED_FIFO with schedutil fed by PELT/WALT. Frequency selection is reactive: "it could take between 50 ms and 100 ms for PELT ... to detect a 90% increase of the CPU bandwidth demand"; WALT "cannot be detected in less than 10 or 20 ms"; against "the buffered audio of only 5.33 ms" (B = 256, b = 64 at 48 kHz). [CLAIMED by source]
  - SCHED_DEADLINE with GRUB-PA and an adaptive runtime fed by an application hint (`workUnits`): "glitch-free low-latency playback at 2.67ms"; CPU energy 96.16 (FIFO+WALT, but latency 26.67 ms) vs 172.71 (FIFO pinned at max OPP, 2.67 ms) vs 102.17 (adaptive DL, 2.67 ms); board energy 206.35 Ws vs 256.93 Ws vs 210.22 Ws on a HiKey 960, SynthMark workload. "a 40% reduction in the CPU energy consumption for the same latency". [MEASURED by source]
  - The mechanism that wins is that a reservation *declares* demand in advance (runtime/period), which the frequency governor can act on before the job runs; a FIFO priority declares nothing. [INFERENCE - mine, from the paper's analysis]

## 7. Mixed criticality and bounding RT in a general-purpose OS

### 7.1 Theory
- The Vestal model: per-task WCET estimates per criticality level; on a LO→HI mode change LO tasks may be abandoned; EDF-VD shrinks HI tasks' deadlines in LO mode by a common factor (Baruah et al.), with schedulability bounds. Burns & Davis, "Mixed Criticality Systems – A Review", 13th ed. (Feb 2022). https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/183619/1/MCS_review_v13.pdf [PROVED by source (reporting the literature)]. A 14th and final edition (Feb 2026) exists: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/238348 (not read). 
- Practitioners object: "In the HI-criticality mode, LO-criticality tasks should not be abandoned"; under ISO 26262 "criticality level is not the same as the importance of the application. Functionality that has low criticality cannot simply be dropped"; standards require "freedom from interference" in spatial and timing domains. Same URL (v13). [CLAIMED by source]
- Lyons et al.: criticality, time-sensitivity and trust must be separate; a budget forces "an untrusted thread to adhere to its declared WCET"; criticality switches are user-level policy, measured fast enough (section 2.3). https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Lyons_MAH_18.pdf [CLAIMED by source]
- Heiser (May 2026): "Temporal isolation is necessary for mixed criticality systems"; timeout exceptions are "Not ideal: better prevent timeout completely"; "Pending RFC against seL4: budget thresholds"; "Functional verification of seL4-MCS due to finish mid-'27"; "Formal schedulability framework due Sep'27"; "Aim: Provable timeliness"; seL4 kernel WCET analysis being revived (Heptane, RISC-V, 64-bit, MCS). https://trustworthy.systems/publications/papers/Heiser_26%3Artas.slides.pdf [CLAIMED by source]
- Composite TCaps: time as a capability carrying *non-replenishable* budget delegated alongside activations and service requests, tracking the priorities of every scheduler on the delegation path; "TCaps, unlike prioritizations and carefully chosen budgets, both meet deadlines for a hard real-time subsystem, and maintain high throughput for a best-effort subsystem". Gadepalli, Gifford, Baier, Kelly, Parmer, RTSS 2017. https://faculty.cs.gwu.edu/gparmer/publications/rtss17tcaps.pdf [CLAIMED by source; MEASURED in the paper's evaluation, not read by me]. Lyons et al. note TCaps "cannot be revoked unless empty" and slack is reusable only by explicit delegation. Lyons URL above. [CLAIMED by source]

### 7.2 How each system bounds RT against everything else
- **Linux**: FIFO/RR bounded by the fair dl_server (5% per CPU, deferred to near zero laxity) since 6.12; SCHED_DEADLINE bounded by admission (≤ 95%); RT_GROUP_SCHED to be replaced by HCBS (per-cgroup, per-CPU dl_servers each running a local FIFO/RR queue; RFC v6 June 2026, "more robust and theoretically sound"; migration and capacity-aware reservations unfinished). https://lkml.iu.edu/2512.0/00651.html , https://lwn.net/Articles/1023943 [CLAIMED by source]. Earlier RT throttling "can force the system idle". https://lwn.net/Articles/934415/ [CLAIMED by source]
- **Fuchsia**: nothing reserves fair time; deadline precedes fair; overload produces "stochastic deadline misses"; critical-deadline over-subscription is a kernel oops. https://fuchsia.dev/docs/concepts/kernel/kernel_scheduling [CLAIMED by source]
- **XNU**: RT has no admission; a compute-bound RT thread is demoted (fail-safe) after `max_unsafe_computation`; Clutch protects lower QoS buckets from higher ones with EDF over WCEL plus starvation-avoidance windows. Section 4 URLs. [CLAIMED by source]
- **Windows**: MMCSS reserves `SystemResponsiveness` (default 20%) for low-priority work; boosted threads that use their quota drop to 1-7. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/multimedia-class-scheduler-service [CLAIMED by source]
- **rtkit**: per-thread RLIMIT_RTTIME kill + a canary watchdog that demotes *all* RT threads on detected starvation. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heftig/rtkit/master/README [CLAIMED by source]
- **seL4 MCS**: every runnable thread holds an SC; admission requires `sched_control`; nothing runs unbudgeted except full SCs (C = T) used as best-effort round robin at a chosen priority. https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Lyons_MAH_18.pdf [CLAIMED by source]
- Pattern: every system whose RT class is fixed-priority-without-budget (Linux FIFO, XNU RT, MMCSS boosts, rtkit) bolts on an a-posteriori policer (throttle, fail-safe demotion, quota demotion, watchdog). Only reservation designs (SCHED_DEADLINE, seL4 SCs, HCBS) bound RT a priori, at admission. [INFERENCE - mine]

### 7.3 The kernel's own bounded work and interrupts
- H-CBS loses its schedulability guarantee when non-preemptive sections or locks add blocking unless the wakeup rule is fixed (Biondi et al. 2014, reported in RTNS 2017). https://retis.santannapisa.it/~tommaso/publications/RTNS-2017.pdf [PROVED by source (reported)]
- seL4 MCS keeps the invariant that every queued thread has budget to exit the kernel, giving scheduler precision of twice the kernel WCET; IRQ-handler threads get time through SC-bearing Notifications. https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Lyons_MAH_18.pdf [CLAIMED by source]
- Zijlstra: IRQ time is not accounted against RT throttling in Linux. https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2306.1/06441.html [CLAIMED by source]

## 8. Latency classes for interactive work without RT privileges

- **latency-nice** (Parth Shah 2019-20; Vincent Guittot 2022): a second nice value −20..19 that "does not change the amount of CPU time a process may consume", only wakeup preemption; lowering it needs CAP_SYS_NICE; "no seeming consensus on the right approach emerged" at OSPM 2020. LWN, Corbet, 2022-03-17. https://lwn.net/Articles/887842/ [CLAIMED by source]

LWN, Corbet, 2024-04-11. https://lwn.net/Articles/969062 [CLAIMED by source]. Clutch's per-class EDF with WCEL states a worst-case scheduling latency per class and bounds higher classes by warp. latency-nice is a relative knob with no stated bound; Windows boosts and Mach decay are heuristics. ## Merits for ToyOS

Everything in this section is [INFERENCE - mine], drawn from the sources above. It is judged on mechanism and evidence, not on who ships it.

### A. Should the RT band become reservations held as capabilities? Yes.
1. **The evidence points one way.** Every system whose RT class is a fixed priority with no budget has had to add a policer after the fact: Linux RT throttling and then the fair dl_server, XNU's fail-safe demotion, MMCSS quotas, rtkit's RLIMIT_RTTIME kill and canary watchdog. Each policer adds failure modes of its own. Linux has shipped two since 6.12: wall-time vs scaled-time charging stalled RT for seconds on big.LITTLE, and the fair server was double-counted against RT admission. Reservations bound RT before anything runs, at admission. Their isolation is proved for CBS/H-CBS (bandwidth α plus a service delay ≤ 2(P−Q)) and kept under shared resources by BWI/M-BWI.
2. **The shape.** Make a *scheduling context* (SC) a kernel object reached by a capability, as in seL4. It holds (Q, P), the CPU it grants, and a bounded refill/replenishment state. A thread is runnable only while it holds an SC with budget. The fair band, the per-CPU dying server and every RT thread are each just an SC. Bands disappear, and so does the dying-task list's ad hoc aging.
3. **Admission belongs to whoever holds bandwidth, and it should be structural.** Treat each CPU's bandwidth as a divisible capability, like Untyped memory: an SC can be minted only from bandwidth its creator holds, so Σ Q/P ≤ 1 per CPU holds by construction rather than by a check. `supervisor` holds the root bandwidth and hands slices to programs from `system.toml` (audio server, display, the fair server's share). The kernel then has nothing to decide. Lyons's "time cannot be hierarchically delegated without loss" argument is about *nested* servers (a server scheduling servers). A flat per-CPU EDF over sibling reservations loses nothing: implicit-deadline EDF is exact at Σ U ≤ 1. So split bandwidth flatly and never nest schedulers.
4. **EDF in the kernel, not fixed priority.** seL4 chose fixed priority so that *overcommitted* systems degrade predictably. ToyOS has no reason to overcommit reservations, because slack goes to the fair band through reclaiming. Under admitted reservations, CBS removes EDF's overload problem: a job that overruns postpones only its own server's deadline. Per-CPU EDF over reservations gives exact admission and optimal utilisation.
5. **Start with implicit deadlines (D = P) only.** Constrained deadlines need the H-CBS-D variants and a PDC or density test (RTNS 2017). Audio periods are naturally D = P. Leave D < P unrepresentable until a workload needs it.
6. **Reclaiming.** A GRUB-style rule (the running reservation consumes budget at rate U_active/U_max) lets admitted work use idle bandwidth and keeps the fair band work-conserving. It is proved for uniprocessors (GRUB) and for multicore global scheduling (Abeni, Lipari, Parri, Sun). Make it optional per SC (Linux's `SCHED_FLAG_RECLAIM`), because audio wants determinism more than throughput.

### B. How audio should be scheduled
1. Each hop on the audio path that runs on its own clock gets its own SC with P = the device period: the driver's IRQ/DMA-completion thread, the mixer, and each client render callback. At 48 kHz, 128 frames gives P = 2.667 ms, and 256 frames gives 5.333 ms. Q is the measured worst case per period. These hops are *asynchronous* (shared rings plus notifications), so donation does not apply, and the mixer and driver must be active servers with their own reservations.
2. Budget in wall time on the CPU the SC is bound to. Scale a request made in "fastest-core time" by that CPU's capacity at admission, as Fuchsia's ProfileProvider does. Never charge in capacity- or frequency-scaled time (the Linux 6.12 bug).
3. Reservations are also the right input to frequency selection. The Android study measured 2.67 ms glitch-free latency at about 40% less CPU energy than FIFO pinned at maximum frequency, because a declared (Q, P) lets the governor raise frequency before the job runs. A per-period work hint, like Android's `workUnits` or Apple's workgroup interval start/finish, lets the client adapt Q without a new admission each period. This links to the energy/capacity work.
4. Give the audio graph one shared deadline object: a "workgroup" capability that the period-start event releases. Clients join it by holding an SC drawn from the audio server's bandwidth, not from their own. Apple's audio workgroups are the precedent. The audio server can admit a client only if its own bandwidth covers it, so an extra client can never cause a 26 ms silence.
5. Portable apps reach this through the existing `audio_thread_priority`/cpal path. The ToyOS fork maps (frames, rate) to a reservation request to the audio server, which mints the SC from its bandwidth. No app changes.

### C. How time crosses IPC to a userland server
1. **Synchronous calls donate.** A call to a server thread blocked in receive with no SC of its own runs on the caller's SC until reply, billed to the caller. Reply objects track nesting, so the chain unwinds correctly. This is seL4's design, measured at +1-4% IPC cost on x86. It makes the immediate priority ceiling fall out, and no kernel thread is needed: the work is charged to whoever caused it. That is ToyOS's own "No kernel threads" principle applied to userland servers.
2. **A busy server inherits.** If the server is already running another client's request, the waiting caller lends its SC to the thread it waits on (bandwidth inheritance, done in the style of proxy execution: the waiter stays queued, and picking it runs the owner). M-BWI proves that non-interacting reservations stay isolated. Interacting ones must budget for others' critical sections (Q ≥ C + I). Fuchsia moved channel calls to exactly this in January 2026, forcing inheritance even for fair threads. Without it, a server shared between an audio client and a fair client inverts.
3. **Budget exhaustion inside a server is a refusal, not a crash.** Give each SC an optional timeout endpoint, as seL4 does. When a donated SC runs out inside a server, the server's handler is told and can roll back or abort the request. The kernel never lets anything run unbudgeted. Heiser (2026) calls timeouts "not ideal" and points to pending budget thresholds (refuse to start a request that cannot finish), which is the cleaner end state for a shared server.
4. **Donation is core-local.** Core-local donation means a server that serves several CPUs needs one passive thread per CPU (seL4's AES result: one migrating server serialises, per-core threads scale). This interacts with placement (D).
5. Plain priority inheritance has nothing to inherit under EDF. XNU's QoS promotion "does not occur across multiple locks". Neither is enough here.

### D. How the fair band is protected
1. The fair band is a per-CPU SC with guaranteed bandwidth U_fair (say 5-10%, a supervisor policy). It is admitted first and never revocable by RT, so RT admission caps at 1 − U_fair − U_irq.
2. It needs no deferral and no starvation monitor. Linux defers its dl_server because FIFO tasks have no deadlines. When every RT entity has a deadline, the fair server running early cannot make an admitted reservation miss, and EDF plus admission already guarantee both.
3. Reclaiming (A.6) plus "fair runs whenever nothing reserved is eligible" make the fair band work-conserving above its floor. That replaces both RT throttling's idle-forcing artifact and the dying-task aging.
4. Charge interrupt and kernel-entry time to whoever caused it. Count non-preemptible kernel sections as blocking (H-CBS must account for them, as Biondi 2014 shows), and reserve U_irq per CPU for unattributable IRQ top halves. Linux's RT throttling never accounted IRQ time.

### E. Per-CPU vs global EDF and placement
1. **Use per-CPU (partitioned) EDF.** It has exact admission (Σ U ≤ 1 per CPU) and no Dhall effect, and it fits ToyOS's existing per-CPU message-passing scheduler. Global EDF gives only bounded tardiness at U ≤ M and needs cross-CPU push/pull for every reservation. Brandenburg & Gül show that semi-partitioned reservations reach 99+% schedulable utilisation with partitioned-level overheads, so global scheduling is not needed for this class.
2. **An SC names its CPU.** Placing a reservation means choosing which CPU's bandwidth to mint it from, which is the supervisor's or the audio server's policy. Migrating one means re-minting on another CPU. The fair band stays freely load-balanced. Only reservations are pinned.
3. **If a reservation does not fit on one CPU**, C=D splitting across two CPUs (semi-partitioning) is the escape hatch. It needs constrained-deadline H-CBS-D, so leave it for later and refuse such a reservation at admission until then.
4. **Asymmetric cores** (P/E, big.LITTLE): admit per CPU in that CPU's capacity units, charge in wall time, and keep the audio graph's SCs on one cluster so donation stays core-local.

### F. What a host simulator should check (tested on the host tier, under ToyOS's "cheapest tier" rule)
Run the kernel's own scheduler and SC code, compiled for the host, under a discrete-event clock. Drive it with generated task sets (randfixedsum/UUniFast) and adversarial threads.
1. **Admission is exact.** No sequence of mint/split/merge operations yields Σ Q/P > 1 on a CPU. Property-test the capability algebra, and make it unrepresentable where possible.
2. **Budget never overruns.** For every SC and every sliding window of length P, consumed ≤ Q + ε, where ε is the documented kernel non-preemptible bound. This must hold with spinning RT threads, IRQ storms and dying tasks.
3. **Zero deadline misses under admitted load.** Every job whose demand ≤ Q completes by its deadline, with service delay ≤ 2(P − Q) plus the blocking bound, including while the fair band is saturated and a hostile SC spins.
4. **The fair floor holds.** The fair SC receives ≥ Q_fair in every one of its periods with RT at maximum admitted utilisation.
5. **Conservation of time.** Per CPU, Σ charged to SCs + idle = elapsed. Time a server spends on a donated or inherited SC is charged to the donor, exactly.
6. **Isolation under sharing (M-BWI Theorem 1 as a differential test).** Add a lock- or IPC-sharing group. Every reservation outside the group has a bit-identical schedule with and without the group. Inheritance chains are acyclic (refused, as Fuchsia refuses with BAD_STATE) and bounded.
7. **Timeout path.** A donated SC exhausted inside a server reaches the timeout endpoint, never runs negative, and the client's request is refused or rolled back.
8. **Overload confinement (test-only bypass of admission).** Misses appear only in the overloaded SC.
9. **Independent oracles.** Use an exact PDC/utilisation analysis written separately, and a differential check against an external analysis (pyRTA implements Prosa-verified analyses). The recorded Linux 6.12 scaled-time failure is a negative control: charge in scaled time and confirm the simulator goes red.
10. **Leave timing to metal.** The simulator asserts order, counts and budgets in virtual time only. Release jitter (cyclictest-style intended vs actual) and audio underruns are metal verdicts.

### G. Open questions the sources do not settle
- Polling server vs sporadic server for replenishment. seL4's sporadic server was found not work-conserving in 2021, and its refill bookkeeping is the most verification-heavy part. H-CBS with deadline postponement is simpler under EDF.
- How big the IRQ reserve is, and whether IRQ time can be fully attributed to driver SCs on ToyOS hardware. This needs a metal measurement.
- Whether interactive fair work needs anything beyond EEVDF slices (sections 8 and the fair-model notes) once audio and display hold reservations.
